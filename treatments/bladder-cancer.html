Understanding Bladder Cancer

What is bladder cancer?

Bladder cancer usually begins in the lining of the bladder. Many cases present with blood in the urine. Care ranges from TURBT and intravesical therapy to radical surgery, chemo, immuno, or radiation.

Causes

Long-term exposure to tobacco smoke and certain industrial chemicals are leading contributors. Chronic bladder irritation and prior pelvic radiation can also play a role.

Risk factors

Smoking is the strongest modifiable risk. Other risks include chemical exposures (dyes, rubber, petroleum), older age, male sex, and chronic urinary infections or stones in some settings.

Importance of early diagnosis

Non-muscle-invasive disease caught early often stays controllable with endoscopic treatment and surveillance. Delaying evaluation of blood in urine can allow deeper invasion.
