Overview

Understanding Brain Cancer

What is brain cancer?

Brain tumours include primary brain cancers and metastases from other sites. Care is led by neurosurgery and neuro-oncology and may include resection, precision radiation, chemotherapy, and molecularly guided therapy.

Causes

Most primary brain tumours have no single known cause. Prior radiation and rare inherited syndromes increase risk. Secondary tumours spread from cancers elsewhere in the body.

Risk factors

Prior cranial radiation, certain genetic syndromes, and older age for some glioma types. Lifestyle links are less clear than for many other cancers.

Importance of early diagnosis

Prompt MRI after new seizures, progressive headaches, or neurological changes can allow safer surgery and earlier adjuvant planning — which affects both survival and quality of life.

Symptoms

Common warning signs

These symptoms do not always mean cancer — but they should be checked by a specialist, especially if they persist.

  • ✓Persistent headaches
  • ✓Seizures
  • ✓Vision or speech changes
  • ✓Weakness or numbness
  • ✓Balance problems
  • ✓Personality or memory changes
Diagnosis

How brain cancer is confirmed

Your care team combines clinical review with imaging, pathology, and staging tests before recommending a plan.

  • ✓Neurological examination
  • ✓MRI of the brain
  • ✓Biopsy / resection pathology
  • ✓Molecular profiling when useful
  • ✓Staging and functional assessment
  • ✓Multidisciplinary tumour board
