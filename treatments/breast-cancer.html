Overview

Understanding Breast Cancer

What is breast cancer?

Breast cancer begins when cells in the breast grow uncontrollably, most often forming a lump or tumour. It may stay local or spread to lymph nodes and other organs. International patients commonly travel for surgery, systemic therapy, radiation, and reconstruction under one coordinated plan.

Causes

Most cases are not linked to a single cause. Changes in breast cell DNA can be driven by ageing, hormones, inherited gene variants (such as BRCA), prior radiation, and lifestyle factors. Many patients have no clear family history.

Risk factors

Risk rises with age, dense breast tissue, early periods or late menopause, hormone therapy, obesity, alcohol use, and a first-degree relative with breast or ovarian cancer. Being a woman is the strongest baseline risk factor.

Importance of early diagnosis

Early diagnosis often allows breast-conserving surgery, shorter treatment, and better long-term outcomes. Prompt imaging and biopsy after a new lump or imaging finding can change the entire care pathway.

Symptoms

Common warning signs

These symptoms do not always mean cancer — but they should be checked by a specialist, especially if they persist.

  • ✓A new lump in the breast or underarm
  • ✓Change in breast size or shape
  • ✓Skin dimpling or redness
  • ✓Nipple discharge or inversion
  • ✓Persistent breast pain
  • ✓Swelling in part of the breast
Diagnosis

How breast cancer is confirmed

Your care team combines clinical review with imaging, pathology, and staging tests before recommending a plan.

  • ✓Clinical breast examination
  • ✓Mammography and ultrasound
  • ✓MRI when indicated
  • ✓Core needle biopsy
  • ✓Hormone receptor and HER2 testing
  • ✓CT or bone scan for staging, if needed
