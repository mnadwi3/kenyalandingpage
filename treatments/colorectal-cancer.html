Overview

Understanding Colorectal Cancer

What is colorectal cancer?

Colorectal cancer starts in the colon or rectum, often from polyps that become malignant over time. Treatment is guided by stage and may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation (especially for rectal cancer), and targeted or immunotherapy.

Causes

Most cases arise from acquired genetic changes in bowel lining cells. Diet, inflammation, and inherited syndromes (such as Lynch) can play a role. Not every patient has a known cause.

Risk factors

Age over 50, family history, inflammatory bowel disease, smoking, obesity, low-fibre/high-processed-meat diets, and hereditary syndromes increase risk.

Importance of early diagnosis

Polyps and early cancers found on colonoscopy are far easier to treat. Early stage often means surgery alone or shorter systemic therapy.

Symptoms

Common warning signs

These symptoms do not always mean cancer — but they should be checked by a specialist, especially if they persist.

  • ✓Change in bowel habits
  • ✓Blood in stool
  • ✓Abdominal pain or cramping
  • ✓Unexplained weight loss
  • ✓Fatigue or anaemia
  • ✓Feeling that bowel does not empty
Diagnosis

How colorectal cancer is confirmed

Your care team combines clinical review with imaging, pathology, and staging tests before recommending a plan.

  • ✓Colonoscopy with biopsy
  • ✓Pathology confirmation
  • ✓CT / MRI staging
  • ✓Blood tests and tumour markers
  • ✓Molecular testing when useful
  • ✓Tumour board review
