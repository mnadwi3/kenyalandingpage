Overview

Understanding Liver Cancer

What is liver cancer?

Primary liver cancer (including hepatocellular carcinoma) arises in liver cells and is often linked to chronic liver disease. Treatment may combine surgery, ablation, embolisation, targeted drugs, immunotherapy, or transplant evaluation.

Causes

Chronic hepatitis B or C, cirrhosis from alcohol or fatty liver disease, aflatoxin exposure, and some metabolic conditions increase risk. Cancer develops after years of liver injury in many patients.

Risk factors

Chronic viral hepatitis, cirrhosis, heavy alcohol use, obesity-related liver disease, diabetes, and family history raise risk. Men and older adults are affected more often.

Importance of early diagnosis

Early disease may still allow curative resection or ablation. Surveillance in high-risk patients and fast staging after abnormal imaging improve options.

Symptoms

Common warning signs

These symptoms do not always mean cancer — but they should be checked by a specialist, especially if they persist.

  • ✓Upper abdominal pain or fullness
  • ✓Unexplained weight loss
  • ✓Yellowing of skin or eyes
  • ✓Loss of appetite
  • ✓Swelling in the abdomen
  • ✓Fatigue that persists

Your care team combines clinical review with imaging, pathology, and staging tests before recommending a plan.

  • ✓Specialist hepatology / oncology review
  • ✓Blood tests and tumour markers
  • ✓Ultrasound, CT or MRI
  • ✓Biopsy when indicated
  • ✓Staging and liver function assessment
  • ✓Multidisciplinary tumour board
